Step 4 · Tat Tvam Asi & Aham Brahmasmi — Chandogya & Brihadaranyaka

"Place this salt in water and come to me in the morning... The salt has dissolved; you cannot see it, but sip from the top, middle, and bottom. It is all salt. In the same way, the subtle truth pervades all existence. That is the Truth. That is the Self. Tat Tvam Asi (Thou Art That), O Shvetaketu!"

— *Chandogya Upanishad* 6.13

The Universal Micro-Macro Identity

In the *Chandogya Upanishad*, the sage Uddalaka Aruni shatters his son Shvetaketu's intellectual pride with simple physical experiments:

- Crack open a tiny banyan fig seed: inside is microscopic hollow emptiness. Yet from that invisible essence springs a giant tree that shelters a hundred chariots.

- Dissolve a lump of salt in a bowl of water: you cannot point to the salt with your finger, but every drop of the water tastes salty.

In the same way, the ultimate reality (*Brahman*) is not a physical object located in a specific coordinate in space. It is the invisible, conscious field that permeates every atom of the cosmos. And Uddalaka delivers the thunderbolt: **"Tat Tvam Asi" — You are not a helpless, isolated biological creature; You are That very consciousness.**

In the *Brihadaranyaka Upanishad*, Sage Yajnavalkya prepares to renounce his wealth to go into the forest. His wife Maitreyi refuses his gold and jewels, asking: *"If the whole earth filled with wealth were mine, would I become immortal through it?"* Yajnavalkya replies: *"No, your life would be just like that of wealthy people. Of immortality, there is no hope through wealth."* He then teaches her that all human love—for husband, wife, children, art, and knowledge—is fundamentally love for the sacred Self (*Atman*) that shines within them all.
