Step 4 · Circadian & Seasonal Health (Dinacharya & Ritucharya)

"He who regulates his food, sleep, and conduct in harmony with the day and season preserves his health and lives a full century of unimpaired vitality."

— *Charaka Samhita*, Sutrasthana 7.60

Dinacharya: The Daily Circadian Clock

Thousands of years before modern medicine won Nobel prizes for circadian clock genetics, Charaka mapped the 24-hour cycle to the dominance of the three doshas:

- **6:00 AM – 10:00 AM (Kapha):** Heavy, cool, slow. Ideal for vigorous exercise, movement, and clearing mucous.

- **10:00 AM – 2:00 PM (Pitta):** The solar peak. Digestive fire is at maximum power. **Eat your largest, most nutrient-dense meal of the day here.**

- **2:00 PM – 6:00 PM (Vata):** Light, agile, creative. Peak time for mental work, writing, and communication.

- **6:00 PM – 10:00 PM (Kapha):** Cooling down, grounding. Eat a light dinner, dim lights, wind down the senses.

- **10:00 PM – 2:00 AM (Pitta):** Internal liver metabolism, cellular repair, and brain detox. You must be asleep during this window; staying awake triggers night cravings and liver stress.

- **2:00 AM – 6:00 AM (Vata):** Subtle, ethereal consciousness. Awakening during *Brahma Muhurta* (approx. 4:30–5:30 AM) grants pristine mental clarity.

Ritucharya: The Seasonal Rhythm

Charaka splits the year into two cosmic movements:

1. **Adana Kala (Northern Solstice):** The sun draws moisture; the environment becomes dry, windy, and depleting. Requires soothing, hydrating, sweet, and unctuous nourishment.

2. **Visarga Kala (Southern Solstice):** The moon cools and clouds release rain. Digestive strength rebounds. During winter, Agni burns hot, allowing heavier, richer foods, while during the rainy monsoon, dampness weakens Agni, requiring warm spices (ginger, black pepper, cumin) to protect gut health.
