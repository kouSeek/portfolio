Step 2 · Pratyahara & Dharana — Mastering the Scatter of Attention

"Give the mind full rein; let it jump as it pleases. Just sit down and watch it. Knowledge is power. After a few days, the mind will become quieter."

— Swami Vivekananda, *Raja Yoga*

How to Tame the Monkey Mind

Vivekananda gives the famous analogy of the monkey:

Imagine a monkey, naturally restless. Now, let someone give him liquor to drink—he becomes hyperactive. Next, a scorpion stings him—he hops about in agony. Finally, a demon enters him—he is completely manic.

This is the condition of the human mind:

- Naturally restless.

- Intoxicated by sensory desires.

- Stung by the scorpion of jealousy and envy.

- Possessed by the demon of pride and ego.

How do you calm it? Not by beating it or suppressing thoughts. suppressing thoughts creates neurosis and subconscious explosions.

Vivekananda teaches **Pratyahara (Sensory Restraint)**:

Sit quietly in the morning. Close your eyes. **Let the mind think whatever it wants.** Do not judge, do not condemn, do not encourage. Simply sit as a detached witness (*Sakshi*).

Thoughts are bubbles rising from the bottom of a lake to burst at the surface. When you stop reacting to them, their momentum slows down. Once the mind is calm, **Dharana (Concentration)** begins: fixing awareness on a single point (the breath, the heart space, or a single noble ideal) until distraction drops away.
